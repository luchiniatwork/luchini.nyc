# Research: Disconnect urgency from importance – How to know when the time is ripe

- Planned post: `2009-10-09-disconnect-urgency-from-importance`

- Status: handed off
- Started: 2026-09-24

Retroactive note for the September 2026 republication. Every linked source was
fetched and read on 2026-09-24. Fixes applied during this pass are listed at
the bottom.

## Research questions

1. Is the "mere urgency effect" real, who authored it, and does the post
   describe the 2018 study correctly?
2. Does the Eagle Hill November 2025 survey support "55% burnout" and
   "nearly three times more likely to plan to leave"?
3. Does the BBC 996 article support: 9am–9pm × 6 days, China's 2021 legal
   crackdown, and the 70-hour vs 50-hour productivity finding?

## Findings

### The 2009 baseline (preserved original prose)

- "Highly-paid executives in the U.S. worked, on average, 58 hours a week" —
  original 2009 claim, source lost to time; the post frames it as
  historical ("When I first wrote this"), which is the right handling.
- Mechanical fix: 58 h/wk × 52 = 3,016 h/yr, so "Almost 3,000 hours" became
  "Over 3,000 hours" (quiet mechanical fix per repost convention).

### Ecclesiastes

- "There is a time for everything, and a season for every activity under
  heaven" — Ecclesiastes 3:1 (NIV wording). Traditionally attributed to
  Solomon; dating ~3rd century BCE makes "more than two thousand years ago"
  safe.

### Mere urgency effect

- Zhu, M., Yang, Y., & Hsee, C. K., "The Mere Urgency Effect", *Journal of
  Consumer Research*, Vol. 45, No. 3, Oct 2018, pp. 673–690 —
  [doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucy008](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcr/ucy008)
  (Oxford Academic). DOI 403s bots but resolves correctly; existence and
  authorship confirmed via OUP, JSTOR (Vol. 45, No. 3, Oct 2018), PsycNet,
  and a Purdue-hosted PDF.
- The post's summary is accurate: participants systematically chose urgent
  tasks over more important ones even when the urgent task paid less and
  its expiration was arbitrary (spurious urgency). Author names in the post
  ("Meng Zhu, Yang Yang and Christopher Hsee") match.

### Eagle Hill burnout survey

- "More than half of the U.S. workforce (55%) is experiencing burnout" and
  "burnt-out employees are nearly three times more likely to say they plan
  to leave their employer in the coming year" —
  [PRNewswire release](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/more-than-half-of-us-workers-report-burnout-new-eagle-hill-research-finds-302624641.html)
  (Nov 24, 2025) and Eagle Hill's own news page. Survey by Ipsos, Nov 2025,
  n > 1,400 U.S. employees.
- Caveat: the post links the [insights/infographic page](https://www.eaglehillconsulting.com/insights/worker-burnout/),
  which says "over half" and confirms the Nov 2025 methodology; the exact
  55% and 3× figures live on the news/PR page. Acceptable as-is; the PR
  link is the upgrade if ever wanted.

### 996 and the productivity ceiling (BBC)

- [BBC](https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/cvgn2k285ypo), "AI gold rush
  sees tech firms embracing 72-hour weeks" (2026). Confirmed in the fetched
  text:
  - 996 = 9am–9pm, six days a week; Silicon Valley AI startups openly
    advertising it (Rilla etc.).
  - China: "By 2021 this chorus of disapproval had become too loud for the
    authorities to ignore – and prompted a legal crackdown."
  - Michigan State University finding, quoted: "an employee working 70
    hours per week has almost no difference in output than an employee
    working 50 hours per week."
- Caveat: the post's "China's courts ruled illegal back in 2021" sharpens
  the BBC's "legal crackdown". The sharpening is defensible — China's
  Supreme People's Court and Ministry of Human Resources issued guidance
  in August 2021 declaring 996 unlawful — but the linked article phrases
  it more softly.

## Yardstick candidates

- 58 h/wk × 52 = 3,016 hours a year of executive work — and still "not
  enough time".
- A 70-hour worker ≈ a 50-hour worker (Michigan State via BBC) — twenty
  hours of weekly theater.
- 9am–9pm × 6 days = the "72-hour week" with a brand name.
- Urgent task chosen even when it pays less (Zhu et al. 2018).

## Disagreements and caveats

  framing; do not modernize it into a fresh claim.
- See caveats above (Eagle Hill linked page vs PR page; BBC "crackdown" vs
  "ruled illegal").
