# Post Anatomy

The archive has five recurring shapes. Pick one before drafting; mixing
skeletons mid-post is how drafts stop sounding like him.

## Archetype 1: The Lesson post (management series)

The backbone of the blog. Examples: *Disconnect urgency from importance*,
*Timeboxing*, *Delegation should be a sign of trust*.

**Skeleton:**

1. **Thesis or hook** (1 short paragraph) — state the tool/idea plainly or open
   on a scene.
2. **Context** (1–2 paragraphs) — why this matters, often a statistic or
   observation.
3. **Personal anecdote** (2–4 paragraphs) — the boss, the team, the family.
   This is the heart; the lesson is proven here, not argued.
4. **The principle extracted** (1–2 paragraphs) — generalize from the story.
5. **Takeaway** — a question trio ("is it really important? will it...?"),
   a checklist, or one closing aphorism.

**Skeleton in the wild** — *Disconnect urgency from importance* (2009):
hook ("an important tool on the leader's belt is the ability to say 'no'") →
context (executive hours) → anecdote (the Urgent boss, "this is for
yesterday") → principle (urgency ≠ importance) → takeaway ("What do we take
home from this lesson?").

## Archetype 2: The Story post (narrative → moral)

Examples: *Start believing in the unbelievable* (Cavallaro), *Creative
survival* (Kamkwamba), *Key to success in China*.

**Skeleton:**

1. **Cold open in the story** — "An American engineer called Rick Cavallaro
   was presented a brainteaser: ..."
2. **The arc** — struggle, ridicule/obstacle, turning point (a patron, a
   discovery), resolution. He keeps proper nouns and numbers: names,
   universities, "470 foot-pounds of torque".
3. **The moral** (1–2 paragraphs) — explicit but earned: "He believed himself
   in the barely believable even when everyone else thought his concept was
   crazy."
4. **Exit question or call to the reader.**

The rule: the story must be *about someone or something specific*. A story
post about an abstraction does not exist in this archive.

## Archetype 3: The Argument post (phenomenon → modern examples → refocus)

Examples: *Are you part of a cargo cult*, *Custom code – the source of all
evil?*, *Agile does not mean chaotic*.

**Skeleton:**

1. **Explain the phenomenon** (2–4 paragraphs) — teach the concept from
   scratch (the WWII cargo cult story runs four full paragraphs before any
   point is made).
2. **Pivot line** — "This might sound like a silly recollection of an
   historical event, but it teaches us a great lesson: ..."
3. **Modern examples** (2–4 short sections) — each one a named, concrete case.
   In 2026 posts these carry links and dates.
4. **The danger / the cost** (1 section).
5. **Refocus ending** — "Quit copying, start focusing": imperative heading +
  2–3 sentences on the root cause.

## Archetype 4: The List post (numbered lessons)

Examples: *10 things I learned after a long EJB3 project*, *12 things I
learned evangelizing Clojure at work*.

**Skeleton:**

1. **Framing** — what the project/mission was, and the split. He prefers a
   honest split over a plain list: "4 things that worked and I would do again,
   4 things that didn't work but could easily be fixed, and 4 things that
   didn't work I wouldn't do again."
2. **Numbered H3 items** — each 1–3 short paragraphs: what happened, what to
   do instead. Items may be blunt ("Never assume that the REPL will be used
   just because.").
3. **Final words** (short section literally titled "Final words" in 2018) —
   humility closer: "Not everyone will pick up Clojure for one reason or
   another. And that's OK."

Numbers in the title are honest counts ("10 things", "12 things") — never
clickbait round-ups.

## Archetype 5: The Personal essay (memory → reflection)

Examples: *Imagination*, *Digital archeology*, *There are no geniuses*.

**Skeleton:**

1. **Confessional hook** — "I am falling in love... No. I am not talking about
   some new girl..."; "In a world full of censorial inputs we are rapidly
   loosing our imagination..." (fix the typo, keep the directness).
2. **Memory in detail** — sensory specifics (the horror book's colors and
   smells).
3. **The trigger** — the thing that made him reflect (NetHack, a conversation
   with his son).
4. **Reflection** — what it means, tied to a value.
5. **Warm sign-off** — see closings below.

## Openings catalog (steal these shapes)

- **Confession:** "I am falling in love... No. I am not talking about some new
  girl..."
- **Bold thesis:** "The relationship between companies and employees is very
  similar to a marriage."
- **Era marker:** "I come from the dinosaur era when email did not exist –
  yep, such an era existed - and I can tell you: it sucked."
- **Story cold-open:** "An American engineer called Rick Cavallaro was
  presented a brainteaser."
- **Recent event:** "Two events from earlier this week ended up sending me on
  an digital archeology quest." (2018)
- **Retrospective frame (2026 updates):** an italic editor's note, then the
  original opening preserved — see the repost convention in
  `ghostwriter-playbook.md`.

Never open with: a definition ("X is a..."), a summary of the post ("In this
post I will..."), or a market trend ("In today's fast-paced world...").

## Closings catalog

- **The exit question:** "Are you ready to believe in the unbelievable
  ideas?" / "Shall we start hammering better? Our fingers will be pleased."
- **The checklist:** "## So remember to" + 3–5 bullets of imperatives.
- **The aphorism:** "The tools have changed. The lesson has not." (2026
  update) / "No matter what you do."
- **The warm sign-off:** "Have a nice imaginative time!" / "Hope to make more
  LISP friends!"
- **The call to reflect:** "Therefore what is our excuse for not working hard
  towards the betterment of our community at large?"

One post, one closing move. Never stack a checklist *and* a question *and* a
sign-off.

## Titles

- **Honest and plain**, often a statement or a question: "Are you married to
  your company?", "Why everything should be in version control".
- **Dash subtitles** for the lesson: "The art of decision-making - There is
  nothing more dangerous than hesitation", "Creative survival – Harnessing our
  resources".
- **Playful when earned:** "West meets East: why does my Red Bull taste like
  tea?", "Falling in love... with LISP".
- **Numbers when it is a list:** "10 things I learned...", "12 things I
  learned...". Counts are real.
- Keep existing titles when republishing — the title is part of the post's
  identity (the 2026 updates kept all three original titles).

## Headings inside the post

- **Sentence case**, short, and they say something: "Timeboxing requires
  guts", "Solving problems sooner rather than later", "The biggest cargo cult
  of them all". Not "Introduction", "Background part 2".
- H2 for the main movements (3–6 per post), H3 only inside list posts.
- Imperative or statement headings beat noun-label headings.

## Length

Archive posts run 300–1,900 words; the sweet spot is **600–1,100**. If a draft
exceeds 1,400 words without being a Story or List post, cut before adding
headings.
