# Phrasebook

Signature phrases, constructions, and moves — each with real citations from
the archive. These are spices: a ghostwritten post should use **three to six
of them total**, never all. Overuse turns voice into parody.

## Signature constructions

### The coined term

Name a phenomenon in quotes, define it once, reuse it all post:

> "It is no surprise that the 'email to everybody' practice has led some
> people to react with what I call the 'black hole' behavior. People with the
> 'black hole' syndrome are those to which you send emails and they never
> reply..."

Also seen: the "cool kids" effect (2018), "agent washing" (2026 update).
Maximum one or two per post. The term should be concrete and slightly funny.

### The scene question

Put the reader inside a concrete scenario with "imagine" + present tense:

> "Imagine a young and eager man, who is planning to propose to his
> girlfriend. They have been dating for several years and he is sure that she
> is The One."

### The pivot sentence

A short standalone sentence that turns the post:

> "But things changed a while ago."
> "However, Kamkwamba had that small spark of creativity that we all have."

### The fold-back intensifier

"...they are six-fold more important in restoring a relationship back to the
level of trust." (2008) — use sparingly, once per post at most.

### The absurd-precision detail

Ground a story with one hyper-specific number or fact:

> "...it inflicted roughly 470 foot-pounds of torque – about the same as the
> V-8 in a new Corvette Z06 – on a drivetrain made of bicycle parts."

The comparison clause ("about the same as...") is part of the move — give the
reader a familiar yardstick.

### The paired folder / binomial joke

Invent two complementary labels for comic effect:

> "He used to say that he organized his received emails in two folders: 'Time
> alone will solve' and 'Time alone has solved'."

### The imperative "Do make sure..."

Teaching mode, softened command:

> "Do make sure this group has easy ways to connect to each other." (2018)
> "Do make sure you know what you will need once you are 'out there'..." (2018)

### The editorial "we" and generic "one"

- "we" for shared experience: "we are rapidly loosing our imagination" (fix
  the typo, keep the construction).
- "one" for the universal person: "One starts to develop the 'black hole'
  syndrome after many years of exposure..."

## Sign-offs (for Personal essays only)

- "Have a nice imaginative time!" — model: "Have a nice X time!" where X is
  the post's theme.
- "Hope to make more LISP friends!" — model: "Hope to make more X friends!"
- "No matter what you do." — standalone last line after the exit question.

These are era-specific (2007) charms. Use at most one, only on a Personal
essay, only when the post is genuinely warm at the end.

## Transitional glue he actually uses

- "However, ..." (sentence-initial, frequent)
- "Indeed, ..." / "Obviously, ..." / "Surprisingly enough, ..."
- "So, ..." / "Therefore, ..." (into the takeaway)
- "On top of that, ..." / "Moreover, ..."
- "In retrospect, ..." (2018)
- "Back then, ..." (2026 updates, for the retrospective frame)

## Words he overuses (keep them, they are fingerprint)

"very", "really", "quite", "simply", "just", "actually", "amazing(ly)",
"incredible/incredibly", "obviously", "literally" (rare). Do not scrub these
out of his sentences; do cap at one intensifier per sentence.

## What does not appear (avoid)

- "leverage" (as a verb), "synergy", "circle back", "double-click on",
  "north star", "unpack"
- "In this article, we will..." / "Without further ado" / "Let's jump in"
- "Buckle up", "Here's the kicker", "Spoiler alert", "hot take"
- Excessive emojis, hashtags, or "TL;DR"
