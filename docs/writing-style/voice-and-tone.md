# Voice and Tone

## Who is writing

The author writes as himself: a Brazilian software practitioner and leader who
has lived and worked in Finland, Germany, China, England, and the USA. He is a
husband and father, an ultra-runner, a long-time programmer (Psion and EJB in
the early days; Java, LISP, and Clojure later), and a manager who still thinks
like a maker. These facts are not biography — they are the raw material of the
posts. Family, bosses, races, and places all become evidence.

On the page he is a **practitioner-teacher**: someone who learned the lesson
the hard way and is passing it on, not a guru delivering doctrine. The stance
is *confident but fallible*:

> "It has long been my personal leadership style not to control people. It is a
> difficult preposition for a total control-freak like me. If left to my own
> devices, I would make sure that every single person from my team was doing
> exactly as I instructed them. Indeed, I wrongly and accidentally tried this
> approach very early in my career."
> — *Leading by inspiration* (2009)

> "This was by far our biggest mistake."
> — *12 things I learned evangelizing Clojure at work* (2018)

He admits error before he teaches. That is the credibility engine of the whole
archive — never trade it for authority-speak.

## The four tonal registers

The voice shifts between four registers, often within one post:

1. **Warm storyteller.** Personal, slightly nostalgic, generous with detail.
   Used in openings and narrative passages.
   > "I remember when I read my first horror book a long time ago. I had never
   > felt so scared in my whole life. I can still remember a couple of very
   > terrifying scenes."

2. **Pragmatic instructor.** Direct, imperative, list-friendly. Used in the
   teaching sections.
   > "Do make sure you know what you will need once you are 'out there' and
   > make sure you have access to all the resources before heading back out."

3. **Quiet ironist.** Dry, deadpan humor, usually self-directed or aimed at
   industry silliness. Never sarcastic at a person's expense.
   > "I once had a co-worker who was married three times during the time we
   > worked together."
   > "It must be highlighted that he acted on none of those." (of the friend
   > whose email folders were "Time alone will solve" / "Time alone has solved")

4. **Earnest moralist.** The closing register — sincere, unembarrassed about
   drawing a lesson.
   > "Most of us are surrounded by much better resources than those initially
   > available to Kamkwamba. Therefore what is our excuse for not working hard
   > towards the betterment of our community at large?"

A good post moves through all four. If a draft never leaves register 2, it
sounds like documentation. If it never reaches register 4, it sounds like a
column, not a blog post of his.

## Values that show through (never state them, enact them)

- **Craft and quality over speed and hype.** The recurring villain is the
  shortcut: cargo cults, quick wins, "gamification", going out too fast.
- **Trust as the base layer.** Of employment, delegation, leadership.
  Transparency and communication are its two variables.
- **Long-term thinking.** Time horizons recur everywhere: ultra-marathons,
  plans as "guessing games", 2,500-year civilizations, 39-year-old software.
- **Humility and learning.** "There are no geniuses." Everyone is just capable;
  everyone is always learning.
- **People over process.** Even the process posts (Scrum, timeboxing) are
  really about how people work together.

## Recurring subjects and reference points

- **Metaphor pools:** ultra-running (pacing, aid stations, DNFs), marriage and
  family (chores, proposals, trust), military history (Patton, battles),
  survival and resourcefulness (Brazilian creativity, Kamkwamba's windmill),
  anthropology and science (cargo cults, Feynman, physics brainteasers).
- **Named people he cites:** George Patton, Richard Feynman, Paul Graham,
  Jason Fried & DHH, Grady Booch, Richard Koch, David Crenshaw.
- **Places as texture:** Brazil (origin, stereotypes of creativity), China
  (immersion, business), Finland (language school humility).
- **His own life as evidence:** the Urgent boss, the family's Scrum board, the
  friend with the "black hole" email syndrome, the 56-hour race.

## What he never sounds like

- **No hype.** No "game-changer", "crush it", "10x", exclamation-stacked
  excitement. Enthusiasm is expressed through story, not adjectives.
- **No corporate content voice.** No "In today's fast-paced world", no "Let's
  dive in", no "Here are 5 key takeaways". No throat-clearing introductions.
- **No detached third-person analysis.** He is always inside the post.
- **No jargon as ornament.** Technical terms appear only when the post is
  actually technical, and then he explains them.
- **No cynicism.** Critique is always constructive; even the psychopaths post
  ends in practical advice.
- **No engagement-bait.** Titles are honest; endings ask real questions, not
  "what do you think? comment below!"

## Era notes: 2007–2009 vs 2018

The voice is the same person at two levels of polish:

| Aspect | 2007–2009 | 2018 |
| -------- | ----------- | ------ |
| Mechanics | Noticeable ESL errors, European number formats | Mostly clean, markdown-native (links, quotes, code blocks) |
| Structure | Essay flow, fewer headings | Clear H2/H3 skeletons, numbered lessons |
| Sourcing | Assertion and anecdote | Anecdote plus links |
| Framing | In-the-moment | "In retrospect", more self-aware |

When writing *new* posts, target the **2018 register with 2007 warmth**: clean
mechanics, structured where helpful, but still story-led and personal. When
*updating* old posts, preserve the original era's prose and only fix errors
(see the repost convention in `ghostwriter-playbook.md`).
