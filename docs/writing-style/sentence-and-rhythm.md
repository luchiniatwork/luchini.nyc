# Sentence and Rhythm

How the prose actually moves on the page. This is the file to keep open while
editing a draft.

## Sentence length: the long-short heartbeat

The default sentence is medium (12–25 words), plain in structure, one idea
each. The signature move is the **punch line**: a 2–7 word sentence dropped
right after a longer buildup, usually as its own beat or paragraph.

> "Cavallaro started to be ridiculed all over the place."
> "But things changed a while ago."
> "Simple!"
> "Wrong."
> "Nobody believed Cavallaro until he bumped into Google's Larry Page..."

Rule of thumb: after any paragraph that builds an argument, consider whether
the point lands harder as a short sentence. He does this two to five times per
post — never more.

## Paragraph shape

- **Two to four sentences is the norm.** Walls of text do not exist in this
  archive.
- **One-sentence paragraphs are deliberate emphasis**, used a handful of times
  per post:
  > "Both armies sent supplies to their bases either by cargo planes or cargo
  > ships." → then the next idea starts a new paragraph.
- Lists are used for checklists and enumerated lessons, not as a substitute for
  prose. A post that is mostly bullets is not his post.

## Openers of sentences

He leans on a stable set of sentence-initial connectors. Rotate among them;
do not stack the same one twice in a row:

- "However, ..." / "Indeed, ..." / "Of course, ..." / "Obviously, ..."
- "The fact is that ..." / "The truth is that ..."
- "It is no surprise that ..." / "It comes as no surprise ..."
- "Take X, for example." / "Another example comes in handy: ..."
- "So, ..." (to pivot into the takeaway)

## Asides and parentheses

Humor and texture live in asides. Three forms, all his:

1. **Parenthetical** — "(a.k.a. “my wife”)", "(yes - you read that right -
   LISPers claim the language was a phenomenon that was 'discovered')".
2. **Dash-joined** — "Email is a great communication tool. But great tools
   have this annoying problem of empowering us to use them wrongly."
3. **Dash-separated clause** — he uses spaced en dashes " – " and double
   hyphens " -- " inconsistently (see mechanics below). Either is fine in new
   posts; pick one per post and stay with it. Em dashes "—" appear in the
   2026 updates and are acceptable.

Asides are for warmth, not for smuggling in complexity. If an aside carries a
load-bearing fact, promote it to a sentence.

## Rhetorical questions

Constant, and doing real work. Three uses:

- **Set up the section:** "What is the key to success in China?"
- **Put the reader in the scene:** "If a question or action point has been
  'assigned' to 57 different recipients... who is really responsible for doing
  it or answering it?"
- **Close the post:** "Are you ready to believe in the unbelievable ideas?"

A post without at least two rhetorical questions does not sound like him. A
paragraph with more than one feels like an interrogation.

## Vocabulary

- **Plain spoken English**, mildly Latinate through the Portuguese first
  language: "endeavor", "paraphernalia", "blissful", "astounding" appear, but
  always inside simple sentences.
- **Intensifiers:** "very", "really", "quite", "simply", "incredibly",
  "amazingly". He uses them freely; do not prune them all — but cap at one per
  sentence.
- **Coined terms in quotes:** he names phenomena and returns to the name all
  post long: the "email to everybody" issue, the "black hole" syndrome, the
  "cool kids" effect, "agent washing". This is his strongest rhetorical
  device. One coined term per post, maximum two.
- **"a.k.a."** and **"rule-of-thumb"** are habitual.
- Domain mixing is the charm: sprint grooming next to aid stations, marriage
  next to org charts. See the metaphor pools in `voice-and-tone.md`.

## Numbers, dates, and formats (mechanics for new posts)

The 2007–2009 originals used European conventions ("3.000 hours", "USD10.000",
"a 2.500-year civilization", "at 3.00"). **Do not imitate these.** The 2026
updates standardized on US-English formatting:

- Thousands separator: comma — "3,000 hours", "$10,000"
- Money: "$40 billion", "USD 80" both acceptable; be consistent within a post
- Dates in prose: "September 2026", "April 2, 2026"
- Big round numbers in words are fine: "a civilization thousands of years old"

## Quotation marks and apostrophes

The archive mixes curly and straight quotes. New posts use **straight double
quotes** for quoted terms and dialogue; curly is tolerated because the source
files already contain it. Never mix within one post.

## The non-native-English fingerprint

He is Brazilian and it shows — mostly as charm, sometimes as error. The
ghostwriter's job is to **keep the charm, fix the error**.

### Keep (grammatical, distinctive)

- Slightly formal connective tissue: "It comes as no surprise", "It goes
  without saying", "In one simple word: understanding".
- Direct, unhedged statements where a native writer might soften: "This is
  simply too special to be ignored."
- Word-order flavor that is correct but marked: "I am more than happy with my
  superb and lovely wife!"
- Present-tense scene setting: "Imagine a young and eager man, who is planning
  to propose to his girlfriend."

### Fix (errors found in the archive — never reproduce)

| Error pattern | Example from archive | Correction |
| --------------- | ---------------------- | ------------ |
| Misspellings | "endevouring", "loosing", "supervisior", "afair", "increadibly", "paralell", "negoatiate", "untill", "firtst", "Pyschopaths" (in a link label) | Spell-check everything |
| Agreement | "an expensive movies", "those words which do not start" (when meaning the opposite) | one noun, one number |
| Verb form | "have being protecting", "I got carried way", "the burnt of a liquid acid", "been used" | being → been, carried away, burn |
| Missing/extra article | "a complete different scenario" | "a completely different scenario" |
| Preposition drift | "put our company into the right track", "hang on air", "confessed me" | "on the right track", "hang in the air", "confessed to me" |
| Dated inclusive forms | "his/her", "businessman or woman" | recast in plural ("candidates... their") or alternate |

### The test

Read the sentence aloud. If a native speaker would call it wrong, fix it. If
they would only call it *slightly foreign*, it is the voice — leave it alone.

## A note on "polish"

Do not over-edit into generic fluency. These sentences are all correct and all
his; a copyeditor who "improves" them is destroying the asset:

> "Hope to make more LISP friends!"
> "Our fingers will be pleased."
> "The badges did not save it."
> "The epilogue wrote itself, slowly."
